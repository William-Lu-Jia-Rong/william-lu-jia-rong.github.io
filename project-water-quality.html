Embedded team project · ECE 198

A mobile platform for accessible water-quality sensing.

Our team built a boat-based monitoring prototype that brings sensing, propulsion, and physical integration into one STM32-controlled platform designed for real-time TDS data collection.

Mobile prototype

Challenge

Bring measurement to the water.

Manual sampling can be slow and expensive to repeat across multiple locations. The project explored a lower-cost mobile system that could collect water-quality data while creating a foundation for future wireless communication and autonomous operation.

System architecture

Sensing, actuation, and control in one loop.

The prototype coordinates the TDS signal path, embedded control, H-bridge motor drive, power, and mechanical structure.

My contribution

Integrate the pieces into a working physical system.

Embedded control

Coordinate sensing and behavior.

Contributed embedded control logic for sensor collection and coordination of the system’s major subsystems.

Electrical integration

Connect measurement, propulsion, and power.

Worked across the hardware interfaces joining the TDS sensor, STM32 controller, motor drive, and supporting electronics.

Mechanical design

Build for the physical environment.

Contributed to the structural design and custom 3D-printed enclosure used to organize and protect the prototype hardware.

Integration detail

Make every interface explicit.

A wiring plan translated the block-level architecture into concrete connections around the STM32. Capturing those interfaces made assembly and debugging more repeatable for the team.

Outcome

A working foundation, with the next steps visible.

The team completed an integrated mobile prototype and identified a practical path toward future Bluetooth communication and autonomous operation. Those capabilities remain future extensions rather than claims about the current build.
